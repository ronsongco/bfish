## 1. Correction: my "zero additional cost" argument for Whisper direct was wrong

I argued across reviews #1, #13, and #14 that Whisper direct translation costs "zero additional
memory, zero additional model load, and zero additional latency, because the model is already
resident and already decoding that audio."

The memory half holds. The latency half does not, and the doc identifies why:

> Direct translation still does not satisfy the product output contract by itself because `bfish`
> must display authoritative source text alongside English.

Whisper's translate task emits English *instead of* the source transcript, not in addition to it. A
product whose defining output is source-plus-English therefore needs **two decode passes**, not one.
The measured directional range of 0.19–0.26 RTF for the pair, against 0.09–0.14 for transcription
alone, is roughly a doubling. That is not free, and my framing was incorrect on a point that
materially affected the recommendation.

Worth preserving the distinction the doc draws, because the two rejections have different force:
the resource case for Whisper direct is still strong — `small` at 479 MB plus large-v3 at 626 MB is
about 1.1 GB against `translategemma:12b`'s 8.11 GB, and two decodes at 0.26 RTF still leaves ample
headroom. What actually rules it out here is **quality**: Korean dropped the station entirely and
changed 3 p.m. to 5 o'clock, and Portuguese rendered *Estação da Luz* as "station of the light."
Two of four fixtures with critical failures.

Keeping it as a measured baseline rather than a replacement is the right call.

## 2. Apple Translation's setup requirement pulls a roadmap dependency forward

`README.md:344-346` notes that `prepareTranslation()` presents the system's permission and download
UI, and that a headless installed-only CLI session cannot initiate a missing download.

That has an ordering consequence worth making explicit. If Apple Translation becomes the live-path
default — and structurally it is the strongest candidate, with no model load, no keep-alive, no
memory budget, and no GPU contention with WhisperKit — then **a SwiftUI surface becomes a
prerequisite for installation**, not just a nicety. The CLI can consume the assets but can never
bootstrap them.

The roadmap places the SwiftUI target at milestone 8, after live capture and Audio Hijack
validation. If Apple Translation wins the live comparison, some minimal setup surface has to arrive
before that, or every fresh machine requires the owner to know the Settings path from memory.

Not urgent, since the comparison has not run yet. But it is the kind of dependency that is cheap to
note now and awkward to discover during milestone 5.

**This is also the measurement I would prioritize next.** Whisper direct is now characterized well
enough to set aside, both Ollama candidates are carried forward, and Apple Translation is the only
remaining path with no evidence at all — while being the one whose structural fit for live is best.
The one-time asset install is the smallest remaining unblock in the translation workstream.

## 3. The variant space was sampled at its two least favourable points

Noting this for completeness rather than pressing it: the two variants tested are the two least
likely to succeed at this task. `large-v3` has a documented translate-task regression relative to
`large-v2`, and `small` is the weakest model in the lineup. `large-v2` — historically the strongest
Whisper variant for the translate task — and `medium` were not tested.

I would explicitly **not** do this now. Three reasons: the two-decode structural cost is
variant-independent and already decides the product-contract question; the `small` failures are
capability failures rather than task-token failures, so a larger variant improving them is exactly
the size-versus-quality tradeoff Ollama already occupies; and the next gate is a real adapter, which
is worth more than a third probe.

It becomes worth doing under one condition: if Apple Translation also fails and resource constraints
turn binding — for instance if the project ever targets a Mac where 8 GB of resident LLM is not
comfortable. At that point `large-v2` two-pass at ~1.5 GB would be the natural fallback to
characterize. Recording it here so the option is not lost rather than proposing it as work.

## 4. One thing to confirm about the new adapter-direct fixture

`safety-adapter-direct-001` uses `sourceText: "[inaudible]"`. The pipeline's `filterReason` bracket
check matches paired delimiters and would classify that as `.annotation`, suppressing it before any
translator is called.

That is the intended production behaviour, and the `evaluationLayer: "adapter-direct"` field exists
precisely to signal that this fixture must bypass the pipeline and call the adapter directly. The
thing to confirm when the harness is written is that it actually honours that field — if the fixture
is fed through the normal pipeline path, it will be filtered, report a pass, and test nothing.

This is the same shape as the earlier finding where the monotonicity guard was validated on a
single-chunk file: a check that cannot fail reads as a passing check. Cheap to get right while the
harness is being designed, easy to miss afterwards.
